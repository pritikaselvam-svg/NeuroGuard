# Problem Statement

People living with Alzheimer's disease and related cognitive impairment
may experience changes in movement, sleep/activity patterns, falls,
physiological state and medication adherence. Caregivers may not
continuously observe these changes.

There is an opportunity to explore a compact, low-cost wearable that
combines multiple sensing modalities with caregiver-facing software.

**NeuroGuard proposes a wearable monitoring prototype that integrates
optical physiological sensing, motion sensing, battery monitoring,
haptic feedback and a caregiver application.**

The engineering problem is to convert low-level sensor measurements into
understandable, actionable caregiver information while maintaining a
clear distinction between measurement, inference and medical diagnosis.
